## Second guide — drafted 28 September, published 29 September 2026

- **Title:** How to Hand Off Localized Image Variants Without Mixing Languages.
- **Reader problem:** A recipient needs to identify the correct approved image for each language and placement without opening files to guess, and needs to see which requested variants are still awaiting review.
- **Difference from existing guides:** The delivery checklist mentions language and variants only briefly; the folder and filename guides address organization in general. This draft gives a placement-by-language matrix, approval states, a partial-delivery manifest, and a recipient check for this specific handoff risk.
- **Guide:** `../guides/localized-image-variant-handoff.md`. The Harborfield Coffee campaign is a fictional worked example.
- **Human review before publication:** Confirm that the intended reader really handles multilingual creative handoffs; check that the example's two placements and two languages reflect a plausible brief, that the English/French copy references and approval states are clear, and that a partial delivery is explicitly agreed. For any real use, confirm destination dimensions/formats, translated copy with a qualified reviewer, regional language needs, and text alternatives with the actual publishing team. W3C language-tag and images-of-text guidance were checked on 28 September 2026.

## Third guide — published 29 September 2026

- **Title:** How to Replace a Published Image Without Breaking Its Links.
- **Reader problem:** A small website team needs to swap an approved image on a live page without leaving the page, responsive variants, social preview, or older references pointing to the wrong or missing file.
- **Difference from existing guides:** Version control covers approval and naming; duplicate cleanup covers safe file removal; export checklist covers file quality. This draft follows a published image URL through reference discovery, staging, cache-aware release, public verification, and retirement.
- **Guide:** `../guides/replace-published-image-without-broken-links.md`. The Harborfield Coffee page and URLs are fictional teaching examples.
- **Human review before publication:** Confirm the intended reader manages website image releases; check whether the example's homepage, social preview, responsive sources, and email template form a plausible small-site scenario. On a real site, verify actual image URLs and uses, CMS behavior, cache headers/CDN invalidation method, approval record, Search Console image performance, alt text, and whether an old URL must remain available. Google image guidance and RFC 9111 were checked on 29 September 2026.

## Fourth guide — publication authorized 30 September 2026

- **Title:** How to Document Image and Font Permissions Before a Creative Handoff.
- **Reader problem:** A small creative team needs to show a client which images and fonts can be published, credited, edited, or transferred without guessing from the final exports.
- **Difference from existing guides:** The delivery checklist briefly mentions licenses. This draft gives a rights register, separates intended uses from transfer of editable sources and font software, and shows a blocked asset in a fictional launch package.
- **Guide:** `../guides/document-font-image-rights-for-handoff.md`. Harborfield Coffee and all permission records in the example are fictional. The user authorized website publication on 30 September 2026; editorial notes were removed from the public article.
- **Human review before publication:** Check that the register fields fit a real reader workflow and that the fictional permissions cannot be mistaken for real approvals. For any real handoff, confirm the specific contractor agreement, license version, attribution placement, font provider terms, destination, and jurisdiction with the rights owner or qualified adviser. Creative Commons and Adobe primary sources were checked on 30 September 2026. Remove the draft's editorial notes before publication.
